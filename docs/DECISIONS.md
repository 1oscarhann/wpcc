### D-001: top-down 3/4 instead of isometric
**Context.** The spec asks for cozy isometric pixel art built from CC0 packs. Kenney's isometric packs aren't pixel art and have no animated characters, Kenney's pixel packs are top-down, and I couldn't find a cohesive CC0 isometric pixel pack with animated characters and monsters.
**Decision.** Top-down 3/4 at 16 px, using Ninja Adventure (CC0) as the primary pack.
**Consequences.**
- Orthogonal combos are visually obvious, there's no isometric depth sorting, and combat hitboxes are simpler.
- It loses the diorama look, and the style leans Zelda-like.
- Reversible at the cost of a renderer rewrite plus new art, so it has to be decided before Phase 3.

### D-002: same-site deployment
**Context.** `*.pages.dev` and `*.onrender.com` are different registrable domains. Safari blocks third-party cookies regardless of `SameSite=None`.
**Decision.** `play.<domain>` (Pages) and `api.<domain>` (Render). Cookies are `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Lax`, scoped to the API host. Fallback: a Pages Function reverse proxy for `/api` and `/ws`.
**Consequences.** Needs a domain (around $10/yr). CORS allows exactly one origin.

### D-003: in-process Realm Rooms
**Decision.** The `RealmRoom` abstract interface (`join`, `leave`, `on_input`, `on_action`, `tick`, `snapshot_for`, `apply_world_event`, `shutdown`) is implemented by `InProcessRealmRoom`. The gateway only talks to `RoomManager`, never to the simulation directly.
**Consequences.** Every room shares one event loop and 50 ms per tick. The Phase 4 benchmark sets the real capacity. Moving rooms to worker processes later means swapping the implementation for an IPC proxy.

### D-004: LISTEN/NOTIFY between worker and API
**Context.** The worker completes timers and spawns bosses, but rooms and sockets live in the API process.
**Decision.** The worker sends `NOTIFY hf_events, '<json>'` after commit. The API holds one dedicated LISTEN connection on the direct endpoint, and if that drops it falls back to polling `notifications` / `scheduled_events` every 10 s.
**Consequences.** No Redis. PgBouncer transaction mode can't LISTEN, which is why the direct endpoint is used (see D-005).

### D-005: Neon direct endpoint
**Decision.** Use the non-pooled endpoint with small SQLAlchemy pools (API about 10, worker about 3, plus 1 listener).
**Consequences.** It supports LISTEN and server-side prepared statements. If the pooler is ever used, asyncpg needs `statement_cache_size=0` and LISTEN has to keep a direct connection.

### D-006: containers + slots
**Decision.** Inventory, bank, equipment, realm stash and mailbox are all `containers`. Items are `container_slots` rows: either a stack, or a single `item_instance`.
**Consequences.**
- One transfer function, one locking rule, one set of ledger entries.
- The mailbox absorbs every "inventory full" edge case (gifts, recovered KO drops, expired listings, quest overflow).

### D-007: milli-units + piecewise weather
**Decision.** Resource amounts and rates are `bigint` thousandths. Weather is a deterministic timeline (realm seed × 20-minute blocks), so lazy settling integrates across blocks and clamps to the cap per block.
**Consequences.** It's exact, cheap (at most about 216 blocks for 3 days offline), and property-tested.

### D-008: two settle paths for timers
**Decision.** The worker settles due events in `due_at` order. Every read or write of a village or job also settles anything already due under the same row lock. Handlers check the target's state before applying anything.
**Consequences.** The game keeps working even when the worker is down. Double processing can't double-apply.

### D-009: favour the player
**Context.** There's no PvP, so leniency costs nothing in fairness between players.
**Decision.**
- Player hits are checked against monster positions at the client's `view_tick` (≤ 200 ms back).
- Monster hits are held up to 100 ms and cancelled by a dodge with `client_tick ≤ hit_tick`.
- Hard anti-cheat stays on everything that touches the economy or leaderboards: speed, cooldowns, range, stamina, pickups, fishing windows.

### D-010: render-only hit-stop
**Decision.** Hit-stop freezes animation playback and the camera for 40–60 ms. The predicted simulation and input sampling keep running.
**Consequences.** Freezing the simulation would desync prediction from the server.

### D-011: shared fixed-step movement
**Decision.** Both sides run `movement.step(state, input, collision)` at 50 ms using only + − × ÷ and `sqrt`. Inputs are quantized identically before use. Golden vectors in `/shared/golden/movement.json` are run by pytest and vitest.
**Consequences.** Reconciliation stays exact, and corrections only happen on real divergence (collisions with moving things, server-applied knockback).

### D-012: secret config sections
**Decision.** `combos.yaml`, secret POIs, hidden lore and hidden achievements never go in `GET /api/config`. Players get per-player views of them.
**Consequences.** The discovery pillar survives DevTools.

### D-013: placement preview endpoint
**Decision.** `GET /api/village/preview` is debounced to 150 ms while dragging. It returns validity, the discovered combos formed, and a `mystery` count, which the client shows as a sparkle.
**Consequences.** One small request per settled hover, rate-limited.

### D-014: fast-forward by rewriting timestamps
**Decision.** `hf fast-forward <user> 2h` subtracts 2 h from that test account's `last_updated_at`, `work_completes_at`, job `completes_at`, and pending `scheduled_events.due_at`, then settles. It's only allowed on `is_test_account` users.
**Consequences.** No per-user clock to thread through every code path.

### D-015 to D-018: art and balance shortcuts
See PLAN §1.3 (P2, P6, P7, P1). Each one trades a little fidelity to the spec for something that's buildable with CC0 art, or that keeps the economy stable across population sizes.

### D-019: imperative Pixi
**Decision.** `Game.ts` owns the Pixi `Application` and frame loop. React components never render Pixi objects. Communication goes through zustand stores (React → game: settings, selected building; game → React: HUD values) and a typed event emitter.
**Consequences.** Predictable performance with no React reconciliation inside the 60 fps loop.

### D-020: mapgen in Python only
**Decision.** The server generates chunks from `(seed, mapgen_version)`. The client fetches them via an immutable, cacheable endpoint.
**Consequences.** There's no cross-language determinism to maintain. `mapgen_version` lets the generator evolve without changing existing realms.

### D-021: fog bitmap
**Decision.** 1 bit per 16×16 chunk (288 bytes per player per realm), with OR-merge updates from the room every 5 s.

### D-022: lazy monsters
**Decision.** Monsters spawn from mapgen spawn points within 2 chunks of a player, run AI at 10 Hz beyond 1 screen, and despawn 60 s after nobody is near.
**Consequences.** This is what makes the tick budget achievable.

### D-024: ledger scope
**Decision.** Every coin, item or resource *transfer* or *spend* writes a row with a reason code. Continuous production accrual doesn't (it's reconstructible from rates and timestamps). `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on `transactions` raise an exception via a trigger.

### D-026: visits via the host's room
**Decision.** A visitor joins the host realm's Room flagged `visitor`, with movement clamped to the host's plot plus a margin. Visitors can't gather, fight or open the stash.

### D-028: persisted KO drops
**Decision.** A KO writes `death_drops` (and its ledger rows) at once, and the room re-hydrates unexpired drops on start. A friend recovering a drop mails the items to the owner.

### D-030: branches
**Context.** The spec wants `phase-N-short-name` branches. This session is pinned to `claude/serene-galileo-iyapi2`.
**Decision.** Phase 1 is committed on the session branch. Phase branches only with your explicit OK.

### D-031: original generated art
**Context.** After Phase 1 you asked for no third-party asset packs ("make the assets") and confirmed top-down.
**Decision.** Every sprite, tile, icon and UI texture is produced by `tools/artgen` (Python and Pillow, deterministic). Characters are drawn as layered parts in shade-index colours and recoloured on the client, which gives the character creator real layers (body, hair, eyes, outfit) instead of palette-swapping fixed characters.
**Consequences.**
- No licence bookkeeping for art, and full control of style and lighting.
- Art quality is bounded by how good the generator code is, so it gets a pass in every screenshot check.
- Adding a building means adding a drawing function.

### D-032: Neon connection-string normalisation
**Context.** Neon hands out `postgresql://…?sslmode=require&channel_binding=require`, and asyncpg rejects both parameters.
**Decision.** `settings.py` rewrites the scheme to `postgresql+asyncpg`, maps `sslmode` → `ssl`, and drops `channel_binding`, so pasting Neon's string just works.

### D-033: plot frame
**Decision.**
- Every plot reserves a 48×48 frame on the realm map. Building coordinates are frame-local and never change.
- The usable area is the centred `size×size` square (24 at the start). Each expansion adds 2 tiles per side, so buildings never move when the plot grows.
- The pond sits inside the starting square, at a frame position set in config.

### D-034: feature gates
**Context.** BALANCE uses planks/bricks from level 3 and rare materials at level 5, but the Workshop crafts nothing until Phase 6 and rares need Avatar Mode (Phase 4+).
**Decision.** `game.yaml → gates` controls these. While a gate is off, the config loader converts each plank/brick back to 2 wood/stone and drops rare requirements, so every upgrade stays reachable. Flipping the gate later needs no code change.

### D-036: full village state pushes
**Decision.** Every change (REST response, worker completion via NOTIFY, fallback poll) sends the whole village state with its `version`. Clients ignore older versions and diff old against new to trigger pop-ins, sparkles and toasts.
**Consequences.** It's simpler and can't drift. A 60-building village is about 6 KB per push, which is fine at Build Mode's event rate. Real patches can come later if visitors make this hot.

### D-039 (open): early pacing
**Observation.** A Lumber Camp L1 makes 1 wood per minute. The Town Hall 2 upgrade (200 wood, 150 stone) right after the first buildings means about 2 hours of waiting in Build Mode alone, which misses the "TH2 in ~20 min" target. The plan assumed hand-gathering (Phase 4) would supply the early wood.
**Proposal.** Keep it until Phase 4 lands. If the Phase 8 playtest still drags, raise L1–L2 production about 2.5× (they're capped by storage anyway) or lower the TH2 cost.

### D-040: overgrown plots and clearing
**Context.** After Phase 3 the village was a big empty field ("looks cheap"). Early wood was also too slow (D-039).
**Decision.**
- At signup, `domain/debris.py` scatters about 70 trees, rocks, bushes, logs and weeds over the buildable square. They're deterministic from the village id and clustered into thickets. A radius around the Town Hall stays open so the first buildings fit.
- Debris is stored on `villages.debris` (migration 0002) and blocks placement.
- Tapping a piece calls `POST /api/village/debris/{id}/clear`: it's removed and its yield (from `debris.yaml`, with seeded bonus rolls) goes into storage through the ledger (`debris.clear`).
- Unclaimed plots on the realm map get the same kind of wild growth from mapgen, flagged so the client hides it once a village moves in.

**Consequences.**
- The map is full from minute one.
- Clearing is a satisfying first-session activity that pays roughly 350 wood, 120 stone and some food and ore. That's what fixes the slow start.

### D-041: village scale
**Decision.** Buildings were redrawn bigger and much more detailed (two-storey timber town hall with a clock tower, thatched cottage, red barn, log cabin, stone smithy, market stalls…). Footprints went to 3×3 (4×4 for the Town Hall and Farm, 4×3 Market, 3×4 Dock) so a 16×24 character reads at a sensible scale next to them. The starting plot grew to 32×32 inside the same 48×48 frame. Expansions now go 32 → 36 → 40 → 44 → 48.

### D-042: light layers
**Decision.** Above the terrain go a tiled multiply layer (lush and dry grass patches several tiles wide), an additive warm "sun" layer, drifting multiply cloud shadows, and a CSS vignette with a warm grade. They're smooth on purpose, the way light works in modern pixel-art games, and they break the tile repetition without adding tile variants.

### D-044: aim as a vector
**Context.** PROTOCOL planned `aim:u16` (an angle). Client prediction would then need `cos`/`sin`, and those aren't guaranteed to give identical results in CPython and V8.
**Decision.** INPUT carries `ax, ay` as i8 (like the move stick). The shared step only uses + − × ÷ and `sqrt`, which are exact in both. Golden vectors (`shared/golden/movement.json`, 800 steps covering walls, dodges, combos, lunges and knockback) are replayed bit-for-bit by pytest and vitest.

### D-045: mapgen bumps before launch
**Decision.** Migration 0003 moves existing realms to mapgen v2 (the Whispering Forest). After launch, a bump keeps the old generator for old realms, as PLAN §2.6 says.

### D-046: no input, no step
**Context.** The first cut repeated the last move when a player's input queue ran dry (PLAN §2.5). The server then moved avatars the client never predicted, and every late packet caused a correction: visible rubber-banding.
**Decision.** A tick without input doesn't step that avatar. Two inputs are consumed per tick while catching up (queue > 2). Prediction stays exact; a hidden tab simply stands still.

### D-047: runtime-synthesized SFX
**Decision.** `frontend/src/game/sfx.ts` builds every sound from oscillators and filtered noise with WebAudio (swing, chop, mine, pickup, coin, deposit, hit, crit, hurt, squish, growl, hiss, poof, KO, potion, level-up). It's original, weighs nothing to download, and is easy to tweak. `tools/artgen/sfx.py` (WAV files) stays an option if we ever want baked sounds.

### D-048: deposit by walking home
**Decision.** Hand-gathered resources sit in your bag (24 slots) until you walk into your own village square, then fly into storage by themselves (up to the storage cap; the rest stays in the bag with a toast). Rare materials stay in the bag. No button, no menu: going home *is* the deposit.

### D-049: safe villages
**Decision.** Monsters can't step into any plot frame (+2 tiles), and a chase that would cross one gives up and walks home. Meadow slimes spawn between plots, and wolves and spiders only in the forest band.

### D-050: where combat numbers live
**Decision.** `combat.yaml` holds the damage formula constants, dodge and stamina rules, and every weapon moveset (sword and spear so far). `enemies.yaml` holds monsters, elites, biome spawn tables and each monster's loot list. A separate `loot_tables.yaml` (nested, weighted) arrives with gear drops in Phase 6.

### D-051: latency test and input limits
**Context.** PLAN §6 says Phase 4 isn't done until it's been tested at 150 ms. `?lag=150` (dev builds) delays every frame by half that each way, with jitter but strictly in order, like TCP.
**Result.** A scripted walk with dodges, checking the server state for every acked input against what the client predicted: **zero corrections and zero mismatched fields at 150 ms and at 300 ms**.
**What the test caught on the way.**
- The first lag simulator reordered frames (Chromium clamps nested timers), which made the server reject "old" inputs and looked like rubber-banding. WebSockets can't reorder, so the simulator was fixed, not the server.
- A delayed clump of inputs could trip the flat 45 messages/s gateway limit and lose an input. Inputs now have their own token bucket (25/s sustained, 25 burst) in the room. The generic limit (30/s) covers everything else. The server queue holds 1 s of inputs and catches up 2–3 per tick.

### D-052: rare materials never go to waste
**Context.** BALANCE §5.3 says each rare material "guarantees its modifier, up to the item's slot count". A Common roll has no slots, so the screenshot run spent an Ironbark and got nothing back. That feels like being robbed.
**Decision.** If you add any rare material, Common's odds fold into Uncommon: the item gets at least one slot and the first rare's modifier always lands. Extra rares still add luck. The odds bar in the forge panel shows this.

### D-053: Phase 6 combo set
**Decision.** Four combos, all forging things that already work in combat:
- Forge + Lumber Camp → spear (Spearwright)
- Forge + Mine → sword (Bladesmith)
- Tannery + Workshop → leather armour (Leatherworks)
- Forge + Mine + Tannery → plate armour (Armoury, the first 3-building combo, legendary-eligible)

The Tannery joins as a TH3 building. Bows, hammers and staffs wait for their movesets (Phase 11). Armour adds defence in Avatar Mode. Plate's −5% move speed is deferred, because a speed modifier would have to enter the shared step and its golden vectors.

### D-054: how station jobs finish
**Decision.** Ingots, planks, bricks and leather land in storage the moment the job is due. They're settled by the same lazy and worker paths as buildings, and may exceed the cap like refunds. Forged gear waits at the station, with a bobbing icon in the world, until you press Collect, because the reveal is the fun part. One job per station (two for a level 3+ Workshop). A busy station can't be demolished.

### D-055: intermediates on
**Decision.** `gates.intermediates_enabled` is now true (D-034's Phase 6 switch). Level 3+ upgrades cost planks and bricks, which the Workshop (TH2) makes. Rare costs stay gated until starstone is obtainable (Phase 10).

### D-056: idempotency keys live in the action's transaction
**Decision.** Spend, sell and buy routes require an `Idempotency-Key` UUID: building place/upgrade, crafting job start, repair, bag/bank expansion, market sell and merchant buy. The key row is inserted first thing *inside* the action's own transaction, and the response is stored before commit. So a key exists exactly when its action committed.
- A replay returns the stored response.
- A concurrent duplicate waits on the first transaction's key row, then replays it.
- A failed action rolls its key back, so the client may retry the same key with a fixed request.
- The same key with a different endpoint or body gets 422.

The web client sends a fresh key on every mutation and, on a network error, retries **once with the same key**. A lost response can therefore never charge twice. The worker prunes keys after 24 h. Tested with five simultaneous requests sharing one key: one effect, five identical answers.

### D-057: what the NPC market buys, and from where
**Decision.** Every stackable good in `market.yaml` is sellable:
- Resources and intermediates come out of storage.
- Monster materials and rares come out of your bag first, then the bank.

Gear isn't sold to the NPC market (salvage it instead, D-054). Selling needs a Market building. Its level adds `sell_bonus` (0/2/4/6/8%) at sale time. Prices are the recalculated ones (P1); a sale never moves the price until the next recalc. The payout rounds down once for the whole sale, so splitting a sale into single units can't farm rounding.

### D-058: the wandering merchant has to be visited
**Decision.**
- **Stock.** Tamsin's cart is derived from `(hub:<realm>, rotation index)`, so it needs no table. Every member of a realm sees the same six slots, one of them a marked-down deal, and the same parking spot. Each 4 h rotation she moves to another spot in the hub.
- **Limits.** Purchase limits are per player per rotation (`merchant_purchases`).
- **Buying.** You buy only while your avatar stands at the cart. The server checks your live room position (within `interact_radius` + 0.75). From Build Mode you can peek at the stock.
- **No arbitrage.** The loader rejects any stock entry that the market also buys whose deal price falls below `min_markup` × the best possible market payout. Buying from her and selling to the market can never profit.
- **What's in stock now.** Rares, potions, resource crates and Recipe Hints. A hint reveals the buildings, never the result, of one undiscovered combo; it's stored as discovery kind `hint`. Blueprints, seeds, pet eggs and decorations join the pools with their systems (Phases 14 and 16).

### D-059: the bank stays home
**Decision.** The bank (60 → 200 slots) only opens in Build Mode; the server refuses bank moves while you're in a room. KO losses only ever touch the bag, so the bank is the safe place for rares. Bag (24 → 48) and bank expansions are a coin sink priced in `game.yaml → containers`.

### D-060: the recalc chain and the coin-supply metric
**Decision.**
- **Recalc chain.** On boot, the API (with an in-process worker) and the worker process each call `ensure_started`. It prices everything if the table is empty and schedules the next `market.recalc` on the 10-minute boundary, de-duplicated by boundary. Each recalc schedules the next one, so after downtime the worker does one catch-up recalc instead of a backlog.
- **Window.** A recalc for time *t* counts sales in (t − 24 h, t] and never after *t*. This matters for backfills and the dev seed; a test pins it.
- **Coin supply.** Each recalc writes an `economy_samples` row: coin supply, wallets, 24 h actives, plus coins created and destroyed since the previous sample. That flow is counted by ledger id, not timestamps, so nothing is double-counted or missed.
- **Where to see it.** `GET /api/admin/economy` (role admin, `hf make-admin NAME`) and `hf economy` show supply over 7 days, and faucets vs sinks by reason for 24 h and 7 days.

### D-061: demo market history is a dev-only command
**Decision.** `hf market seed --yes` writes 48 h of made-up realm sales, with a small story per item (a selling spree, a drought, or calm), and recalcs at every step, so sparklines and trend arrows have something to show in screenshots. It refuses without `--yes` and on `HF_ENV=prod`.

### D-062: the tutorial is server-checked and skippable
**Decision.** `config/tutorial.yaml` holds Juniper's eight steps: welcome → Lumber Camp + Quarry → chop 8 wood → bop 2 slimes → build a Market and sell → Forge beside the Lumber Camp (the combo) → forge a spear → done. `users.tutorial_step` is the current index (−1 = finished or skipped).
- **Server-checked.** The client only ever asks `POST /api/tutorial/check`. The server compares each goal against real state (buildings placed, `user_stats` counters, discoveries) and pays step rewards into storage through the ledger (`tutorial.reward`). Several steps can complete in one check.
- **Skippable.** Skipping works at any step and is final.
- **Budget.** The Playwright e2e plays the whole thing through the real UI in **3.5–4.1 minutes** (bot speed, under the 5-minute budget). A human reading the lines will be closer to 5; see the tuning notes.

### D-063: user_stats counters
**Decision.** A generic `user_stats (user_id, stat_key, value)` table. Increments are upserts, so they can't lose a count.
- **Room counters.** `gather.<item>`, `kill.<monster>` and `kill.any` ride the room's write-behind flush.
- **Service counters.** `sell.<item>`, `sell.any`, `sell.coins`, `forge.<gear>`, `forge.<rarity>` and `forge.any`.
- **Who reads them.** The tutorial now; quests and achievements in Phase 16.

### D-064: "While you were away" is a diff, not a log
**Decision.** When a player's last socket closes, we snapshot their settled village: resources, coins, building levels. Coming back after `away.min_absence` (10 min), `GET /api/away-summary` settles again and reports:
- what was gained, and which stores are full;
- buildings finished, goods crafted, gear waiting;
- the coin change, whether the merchant moved, and one of Juniper's quips.

The card clears on acknowledge. Fast-forward ages the snapshot too, so test accounts see it. **Known gap:** an instant reload can open the new socket before the old one closes, so no snapshot is taken. That only skips the card for an absence that was too short to show one anyway.

### D-065: realms as built
**Decision.**
- **Found.** Founding turns your solo realm into a shared one, adding a name, a 6-character invite code (no 0/O/1/I) and a stash.
- **Join.** Joining moves your whole village to the lowest free plot. Buildings are plot-frame relative, so nothing inside moves; only `realm_members`, `villages.realm_id` and the avatar's realm change. Your empty solo realm is archived.
- **Leave or kick.** Either brings back your archived solo realm (or a fresh one) on plot 0.
- **Owner.** The owner can't leave while others remain; they hand over first. The last member leaving just makes the realm solo again.
- **After any move.** Moved players are evicted from the room (`ROOM_LEFT moved`), the room reloads every village's collision, and every member gets a `NOTIFY realm` so their client refetches. A client whose own realm changed reloads the page, because the whole world (seed) is different.
- **Known gap.** A relocated village keeps its old plot's debris layout, so the new plot's path may run under it.

### D-066: the stash trust rules (P3) as built
**Decision.** One `realm_stash` container per shared realm (60 slots). Only from Build Mode, like the bank.
- **Deposit.** Anyone, always.
- **Withdraw.** Unlimited for the owner and trusted members. Up to 400 coins' worth per UTC day for others, valued at market base, 10 per unit for unpriced items, or tier × 60 for gear. Members in their first 48 h can only deposit.
- **Logging.** Every move is in the ledger (`stash.deposit` / `stash.withdraw` against the stash container) and the activity feed. Today's withdrawn value is computed from the ledger, so there's no counter to drift.

### D-067: exploration (fog, waystones, maps)
**Decision.**
- **Fog.** One bit per 16×16 chunk per player per realm (`fog_reveals`, 288 bytes). The room reveals a 5×5-chunk square around you as you move, and the client applies the same rule locally so the map updates instantly. Hub and claimed plots always show.
- **Waystones (P12).** 11 per realm, placed by layout maths alone, in the same places in every realm: Wellside by the well, then a meadow stone and a forest stone beside each forest trail, each with a little clearing. Walk within 2.5 tiles to attune (a `waystone` discovery). Then from any attuned stone you can travel to any other: not mid-fight, not while KO'd, with a second of i-frames on arrival. Mapgen bumped to v3; the migration moves existing realms in place because the change is additive.
- **Maps.** `GET /api/realm/overview` sends one byte per chunk (water, grass, forest, path, hub, plot), your fog and the waystones. The corner mini-map is a local window around you. The full map (M) shows the whole realm, with plot owners, waystones, the merchant and your party, and doubles as the travel picker at a stone.

### D-068: parties are in memory
**Decision.** As D-025 planned, parties live in the RoomManager (lost on restart, simply re-formed).
- **Forming.** Up to 4. Invite a player in the room, and they accept within 60 s.
- **Updates.** Party frames go out once a second as a `PARTY` event: HP, level, position for map dots.
- **Shared XP.** Party members within 16 tiles of a kill get its XP even if they didn't swing. Loot stays per player.
- **Deferred.** Co-op monster HP scaling waits for Phase 13 with the bosses.

### D-069: seeing your realm-mates
**Decision.** `GET /api/realm/villages` returns mates' village states. The client draws their buildings, debris and a name sign read-only, and subscribes to each mate's `VILLAGE_STATE` (now allowed for same-realm members), so their upgrades appear live. Collision already covered every member's village. Build Mode's camera can pan 70 tiles past your plot once you have mates (28 when solo).

## Phase 10: the wider world

### D-070: the world clock is pure maths
**Decision.** Day/night, seasons and weather are pure functions of wall time (`domain/worldclock.py`). Nothing is stored and no worker runs.
- **Day.** One global 24-minute day: dawn 2 m, day 14 m, dusk 2 m, night 6 m. Seasons are 7 days each, starting from 1970 UTC. Every realm shares the sun.
- **Weather (P9).** Per realm, in 20-minute blocks. Block `i` hashes `(realm.weather_seed, i)` and picks a state using the season's weights, with the season read at the block's midpoint. So the forecast is just the next few blocks.
- **Client.** It computes the day phase locally from the clock config in the bundle. `GET /api/world` gives season, weather, effects and a 4-block forecast, refetched when the block ends.

### D-071: weather-aware lazy production
**Decision.** `settle_weather` settles a resource segment by segment (weather blocks), at `round(base × multiplier)`.
- **Merging.** Adjacent equal multipliers are merged first. A resource that no weather touches still settles in one step, exactly as before.
- **Drift.** A sub-unit remainder at a block boundary is dropped: less than one unit per boundary. A test checks that settling in steps matches settling in one go within that bound.
- **Effects.** Rain: food ×1.2, herbs ×1.15. Snow: food ×0.85. Storm: food ×1.1.

### D-072: mapgen v4 rings
**Decision.** Outer rings at radius 175 (forest), 215 (stonepeak), 255 (swamp), 295 (frozen) and 335 (ashen).
- **Edges.** Edges are warped by noise (±18) and dithered (±3) so they never look like circles.
- **Ground and pools.** Each ring has its own ground (rock, mud, snow, ash) and pools: water, murky water, walkable ice, or solid lava.
- **Tile ids.** Ids are one byte, so ice and lava use 16 cardinal-only masks, with the corner bits implied when both sides are set. Without that, interior tiles get notched corners and frozen lakes look like wallpaper.
- **Waystones.** 12 new ones, three per outer ring, appended so the old indices keep their meaning.
- **Migration.** 0007 moves v3 realms to v4 in place.

### D-073: night, fog and storms in combat
**Decision.**
- **Night.** It buffs spawns at spawn time: HP ×1.25, damage ×1.15, entity flag 16 for a violet haze. Kills give loot quantity ×1.5 (stochastic rounding) and rare-material drop chances ×2. Each chunk also gets `groups × 1.4` spawn points. The extra ones are night-only, and they fade at dawn (DEATH with flag 2) unless they're busy fighting.
- **Fog.** Aggro radius ×0.7.
- **Storms.** Each chunk near a player has a 25% chance per weather block of a Storm Elemental. It is a *roaming* enemy: difficulty-1 stats scaled by the biome it appears in. It fires chain lightning (EV ZAP), drops storm glass, and fades when its storm ends.
- **Moonpetals.** These nodes are night-only. The server refuses them by day and the client hides them. A snow block gives frost spires ×1.5 yield (`rare_node`).

### D-074: archetypes on one state machine
**Decision.** All seven archetypes share the hidden → idle → chase → windup → active → recover → return machine.
- **Chargers and tanks** are pure config: a long telegraphed lunge, or a slow slam around themselves.
- **Kiters** telegraph a line, then fire a `Shot` along it. Trees and rocks are cover. A dodge passes through the shot (the HIT event says "dodged"). Kiters back away inside `keep_away`, but for at most 2 s before they shoot anyway.
- **Summoners** call minions every 6 s, up to 4 at once. Minions drop nothing, give 25% XP, and fade when their summoner dies. A summoner's attack is a circle placed on your spot that *roots* you.
- **Root.** The server zeroes movement (self flag 16) and the client predicts none while the flag is set. The few ticks before the flag arrives are fixed by normal reconciliation.
- **Big Slime** splits into two angry slimes that share its spawn point.
- **Deferred.** Elites' "one extra attack".

### D-075: co-op HP scaling at spawn
**Decision.** A monster's HP is ×(1 + 0.5 × extra players within 20 tiles of its spawn point) when it spawns. Minions don't scale. This closes the general part of D-068's deferral; boss scaling stays with Phase 13.

### D-076: the sky is one multiply pass
**Decision.** A screen-space layer between the world and the UI.
- **Colour.** The base colour is season tint × weather tint × the dusk/dawn glow, blending toward night blue.
- **Light.** Additive light sprites (your lantern, lit buildings, lamps, waystones, lava, glowing nodes) are rendered with the base into a half-resolution render texture, then multiplied over the world. In daylight a plain tinted quad is used instead, so day costs nothing extra.
- **Weather.** Precipitation depends on the ring: rain becomes snow in the north and ash in the wastes. Fog banks drift across, and storms bring lightning flashes and thunder.
- **Sound.** The soundscape is synthesized like the SFX: wind and rain beds plus biome critters. It follows the Music slider (now "Music & ambience").
- **Screenshots.** In dev, `?sky=night,storm,winter` (or `window.__hfSky(...)`) overrides the sky.

### D-077: points of interest
**Decision.** Mapgen places POIs per ring from the realm seed (47 per realm with the shipped config).
- **Placement.** Each is on dry land, off trails, roads and waystones, at least 16 tiles from the next, with a clearing around it.
- **Saving.** Using one sends `INTERACT [index]`. What you've used is a `discoveries` row of kind `poi` keyed by `"x,y"` (not the index, so changing counts never mixes up chests), with `data.at`. No migration was needed: the enum value already existed.
- **Ruins** hide a chest: one-time loot per player (coins, ring materials, rare chances).
- **Shrines** give a 10-minute blessing (might, ward or fortune, fixed per shrine), then rest for 20 h. The blessing lives in memory and is restored from `data.at` when you come back.
- **Lore stones** show a piece of original lore and give 15 XP the first time (also a `lore` discovery).
- **Caves** are sealed until dungeons (Phase 13).
- **Traders' stalls** are merchants with id `wild:<index>`. They use the hub merchant's stock rules and limits, with their own stock and a fixed spot.
- **Lost villagers** give coins and XP and bump `villagers.found`. They actually settle in your village in Phase 14.

### D-078: dev teleport
**Decision.** `POST /api/dev/teleport {x, y}` exists only when `HF_ENV=dev`. It moves your avatar to the nearest open tile, heals you and gives 10 s of i-frames. It's for screenshots and playtesting the far rings. In any other environment it returns 404.

## Phase 11: the full weapon roster

### D-079: per-player movement rules instead of new Motion fields
**Decision.** Plate armour (−5% speed, −10% dodge distance), swift food (+8% speed) and the stamina potion (×1.5 regen) change *your* movement.
- **How.** The shared Motion state and the golden vectors stay untouched. The server keeps a per-player copy of the rules (`rules.scaled`: three multiplications of `speed_tick`, `dodge_step` and `st_regen`) and sends the three multipliers as `MOVE_MODS`. The client rebuilds the identical object with the same multiplications, so prediction stays bit-exact.
- **Cost.** A buff starting or ending mispredicts for at most one round trip, which reconciliation smooths out.

### D-080: one move schema for every weapon
**Decision.** Light, heavy and ability moves share one schema. The shapes are `arc`, `line`, `circle` (around you, with `hits`/`interval` repeats), `shot` (projectiles with `speed`, `pierce`, `aoe`, `homing`, `count`/`spread` fans), `starfall` and `barrier`.
- **Client.** It only ever needs the timings, dash and costs for prediction. Everything else is server-side, plus a `sprite`/`impact` hint for visuals.
- **Weapon behaviour.**
  - **Hammers and maces:** slam in circles; their quakes stun (elites take 40%).
  - **Bows:** fan volleys and fire charged piercing shots.
  - **Crystal staff:** bursting orbs, plus a crystal wall that eats monster shots.
  - **Harpoon:** pulls monsters in, never past you.
  - **Arcane bow:** homes and calls down starfall.
- **Spear throw** is now just a `shot`.

### D-081: monster statuses
**Decision.** Burn and poison deal a share of the hit that applied them, as damage over time (poison stacks ×3). Slow is ×0.65 speed. Stun freezes the AI and breaks a wind-up.
- **Kill credit.** Damage over time ticks once a second and credits whoever applied it, so a burn that finishes a monster still gives you the kill.
- **Sources.** Weapon moves (`status` + `proc`), the tome, and the Phase 10 rare materials as forging modifiers: Venom (swamp venom), Frost (frost shard), Fire (ember core) and Lifesteal (moonpetal). Chain Lightning arrives with Ancient Runes.

### D-082: offhands on F (P19)
**Decision.** A new input bit (`offhand`, 32). All offhand work is server-side, so no prediction changes.
- **Shield.** Always adds defence by tier. Holding F with no attack or dodge running blocks 60% of a frontal hit (120° cone) for 10 stamina; the hit shows "blocked!".
- **Barrel Bombs.** Lob 3.5 tiles (stopping at walls), 1.2 s fuse, 45 damage in r2. Three charges that refill one by one, every 6 s.
- **Elemental Tome.** A homing spell every 7 s. Its element comes from the rare material forged into it (frost slows, venom poisons, anything else burns).
- **State.** Charges and cooldowns reach the HUD as `OFFHAND` events.

### D-083: consumables brew at stations; quick-slots 1–4
**Decision.** Potions and food are combo-unlocked recipes like gear, brewed in batches and collected into your bag (`kind = consumable`). The healing potion needs the Apothecary at level 3.
- **Quick-slots.** Equipment slots 4–7 are quick-slots used with 1–4 (`USE_QUICK`). R still drinks the first healing potion.
- **Cooldowns.** Shared per group: potions 8 s, stamina 15 s. Only one food buff at a time.
- **Display.** Buffs show as chips under the minimap.

### D-084: the thirteen combos
**Decision.** Three new buildings (Alchemist at TH2, Wizard Tower and Tavern at TH3) complete the 13 combos from BALANCE §5.4–5.6. Bread (Tavern) and potion base (Alchemist) are new intermediates.

| Combo | Buildings | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Spearwright | forge + lumber camp | spear, bow |
| Bladesmith | forge + mine | sword, shield |
| Hammerhall | forge + quarry | warhammer, mace |
| Crystalcutter | wizard tower + quarry | crystal staff |
| Runesmith | forge + wizard tower | enchanted sword |
| Harpooner | fishing dock + forge | harpoon |
| Starbow Loft | forge + lumber camp + wizard tower | arcane bow |
| Powder Cellar | tavern + workshop | barrel bombs |

- **Legendary.** The arcane bow and tome (both 3-combos) are Legendary-eligible.
- **Arcane bow copper bill.** The arcane bow got a copper bill after all (a 3-combo at level 1 is tier 1).

### D-085: `LOADOUT` and the dev kit
**Decision.**
- **`LOADOUT`.** Equipping over REST now sends the room client a `LOADOUT` event (weapon, offhand, defence), so the predictor switches movesets and the HUD updates without a reload.
- **Dev kit.** `POST /api/dev/kit` exists only when `HF_ENV=dev`. It sets the Town Hall level, fills storage and hands you one of every weapon and offhand plus potions, for playtests and screenshots.

## Phase 12: dungeons ("delves")

### D-086: delves live inside the realm room
**Decision.** A delve is an instance inside your realm's `RealmRoom`, not a separate room. Its tiles sit far east of the map: instance *i* lives at `x = space.x0 + i × space.slot` (4096 + i × 128).
- **Collision.** `WorldGrid.solid` hands any column past `dungeon_x0` to `DungeonSpace.solid`. The client grid has the matching `setDelve` overlay, so prediction stays exact.
- **Separation.** Slots are 128 tiles apart, far wider than the area of interest, so two parties never see each other.
- **Monsters.** They carry their instance id (`Monster.dungeon`). Wild monsters ignore players in delves.
- **Why.** The 20 Hz sim, combat, loot, parties, buffs and persistence all work unchanged. A second room type would have meant a second copy of all of it, plus a room handoff on every enter and exit.
- **Cost.** Every delve ticks in its realm's room, which caps how many can run at once per realm. That's fine at our scale: an empty instance closes after `idle_close` (60 s).

### D-087: the room-graph generator
**Decision.** `domain/dungeon.py` lays rooms on a 4×3 grid of cells (22×18 tiles each).
- **Graph.** A seeded spanning tree joins 9 of the 12 cells, plus one extra edge so there is a loop. Each edge becomes a corridor with a three-tile door at each end.
- **Roles by depth (BFS from the entrance).**
  - The deepest room is the **boss** room, and one room on the main path to it holds the **mini-boss**.
  - Dead ends become **treasure** rooms.
  - Up to two main-path rooms become **puzzles** (plates or levers). Each locks the door leading deeper until it's solved.
  - The rest are **combat** rooms.
- **Seeds.**
  - `seed_for(*parts)` is a blake2b hash, so a seed never depends on Python's per-process hash randomisation.
  - Caves reseed every UTC day from (realm seed, cave key, day).
  - The daily delve uses `seed_for("daily", day)`.
- **Tests.** Layout invariants: everything reachable, exactly one boss, doors on walls, props on floor.

### D-088: rooms and puzzles
**Decision.**
- **Combat and mini-boss rooms.** Walking in spawns the pack and drops every gate of the room. The gates lift when the last monster dies, granting `xp.room × difficulty`. A player standing in a gate when it closes is nudged out.
- **Plates.** Step on the runes in order. A wrong plate resets them with a fizzle.
- **Levers.** Each lever flips a fixed set of lamps (a bitmask). All lamps lit solves the room. The masks are generated so the puzzle is always solvable, and a test brute-forces it.
- **Treasure chests.** Each player can open each chest once, for coins, a chance of an Ancient Rune and a chance of a blueprint.
- **HUD counter.** It shows rooms *explored* (visited / total), not "cleared". Treasure and puzzle rooms never had a pack to clear, so the old counter showed 1/9 after a full clear.

### D-089: the Delve Warden
**Decision.** Every delve ends at the Delve Warden, a 420 HP obsidian tank.
- **Attacks.** A circle slam up close, and a line charge (`alt_attack`, lunge 5.5) when you're 2–7 tiles away. `alt_attack` is a new optional field on any enemy.
- **Phases.** It calls two adds at 66% and again at 33%, and enrages below 25% (×1.35 speed).
- **Health bar.** The boss entity carries flag 64, so the client draws a bar for it.
- **Phase messages.** "Wakes", "calls for help" and "is enraged" show under the boss bar, which turns red on enrage, instead of as toasts. Three toasts stacked over a small room hid the fight.
- **Art.** The generic biped scaled ×2.2 read as a box on stick legs. The warden now has a hand-built sprite (`bestiary.warden`): pauldrons, stone fists, a glowing chest rune, and windup and slam poses.

### D-090: the daily delve and its leaderboard
**Decision.** The Delve Gate stands in every hub (`daily.place`, an offset from the well). Its delve is seeded by the UTC date, so it is the same layout for every realm.
- **Recording.** Killing the boss opens a portal home and records a `dungeon_runs` row: kind, key, day, seed, party names and ids, clear time and deaths. The row is saved on the room's next flush.
- **Board.** `GET /api/dungeons/daily` returns the day's fastest clears (top 20) and your own best.
- **Schema.** As built, the table keys runs by `(kind, day, clear_ms)` with text keys. The `is_daily`/`party uuid[]` sketch in SCHEMA §9 was replaced; uncleared runs aren't stored at all.
- **Rewards.**
  - **Ancient Runes.** They come only from delves (P15). They are a forging modifier and sell at the market.
  - **Blueprints.** Four decor blueprints drop from treasure and the boss. They can't be used until Phase 14 (decor). Until then they're collectibles that sell.

### D-091: getting in and out
**Decision.**
- **Entering.**
  - Use a sealed cave in the wild (that ring's delve) or the Delve Gate (the daily delve).
  - Party members join the same instance, up to `max_party` (4). A solo player gets their own instance.
- **Leaving mid-run.** It takes a second click ("Really leave?") and is refused while you're combat-locked.
  - **Why the confirm.** Leaving abandons the run: coming back starts a fresh instance.
- **After the boss.** "Head home" works from anywhere in the delve. Before, it only worked standing on the portal, and the button silently did nothing.
- **Knockouts.** A KO drops you out of the delve and counts a death on the run. You respawn as you would anywhere else.
- **Disconnects.** A disconnect also drops you out. You come back at the entrance you came in by, because your saved position is always your outside spot (`dungeon_return`).
- **Dev routes (dev only).** `POST /api/dev/delve-weaken` knocks every monster in your delve down to a share of its HP, so the screenshot run reaches the portal. `/api/dev/teleport` works inside delves.

## Phase 13: defence and bosses

### D-092: party scaling for bosses
**Decision.** Realm bosses and the Delve Warden scale with the fighters around them: HP × (1 + 0.75 × (fighters − 1)).
- **Fighters.** Players within 14 tiles who aren't knocked out.
- **Rechecks.** Realm bosses recheck every second and keep the share of HP they have left, so leaving can't skip HP. The Warden is scaled once, when it spawns, from the party in the delve.
- **Correction.** In the Phase 12 wrap-up I said bosses didn't scale with party size at all. That was wrong: every spawn already had the generic co-op bump (+50% per extra player within 20 tiles). Bosses now use the steeper 0.75.
- **The daily board.** It's still not perfectly fair between solo players and parties. Splitting it by party size is the cleaner fix and is noted for Phase 17.

### D-093: realm boss loot
**Decision.** When a realm boss falls, everyone who dealt at least 1% of its damage and is still in the realm gets their own drops: XP, coins, the boss's biome rares, and maybe its trophy.
- **Trophies.** Your first kill of each boss always drops its trophy (checked against your `kill.<boss>` stat). After that the chance is 20%.
- **Tally.** Damage is tallied per player and saved to `boss_damage` as it changes. A restart doesn't wipe the tally, but the boss's HP rolls back up to 10 seconds (its HP share is only saved every 10 s).
- **Leaving early.** Players who left before the kill get nothing. There's no mailbox to post loot to until Phase 15.

### D-094: boss HP retuned to the damage we actually have
**Decision.** Solo HP is 2,400 (Grubmaw), 5,000 (Colossus), 8,000 (Hydra), 11,000 (Matriarch) and 15,000 (Tyrant), instead of BALANCE §7's 7,500–60,000.
- **Why.** As built, a starter sword hits for about 12, and tier gear for a few times that. The plan's numbers would have meant 15–20-minute solo fights against the 2–4-minute target.

### D-095: data-driven bosses
**Decision.** `bosses.yaml` drives a `BossBrain` on top of an ordinary `Monster`.
- **Phases.** Each phase starts at an HP share and has its own attack rotation, speed and damage multipliers.
- **Attack shapes.**
  - `circle`, and `line` (a charge when it has a lunge)
  - `rain`: circles dropped around every fighter, one right on you
  - `cone`
  - `ring`: a donut, safe inside it and outside it
  - `lines`: cracks radiating out from the boss
- **Mechanics.**
  - Adds are summoned when a phase starts.
  - The Hydra regrows unless it's burning.
  - The Colossus's core is exposed (×2 damage) after a fist slam.
  - The Matriarch's blizzard turns the sky to snow around her.
  - The Tyrant enrages.
- **Immunities.** Bosses ignore stuns and heavy-hit staggers, and shrug off 80% of knockback.
- **Telegraphs.** Every hazard gets its own telegraph id, so several can show at once. Telegraphs gain shape 2 (ring) and shape 3 (cone).
- **Phase notes.** They show under the boss bar, as with the Warden (D-089).

### D-096: when and where bosses wake
**Decision.** In every realm with someone online, a boss wakes every 72 hours and waits 24 hours. A realm's first boss wakes 15 minutes after its first visit.
- **Rotation.** It cycles through the bosses whose `unlock_level` the realm's best avatar has reached. A fresh realm only meets Grubmaw.
- **Lair.** The lair is a seeded spot on the boss's biome ring.
- **Persistence.** The room loads waiting bosses when it starts and every 30 seconds after. It saves the boss's HP share every 10 seconds and damage tallies whenever they change.
- **Leashing.** A boss nobody is fighting walks home and heals.
- **Map.** Awake bosses show as a red diamond on both maps, even through the fog: the whole realm hears it wake.

### D-097: raids follow the online rules (P18)
**Decision.**
- **The director.** A director in the API process checks every 10 seconds.
- **Online.** A live socket, or one that dropped less than 60 seconds ago.
- **When a raid is announced.** A village qualifies when all of these hold:
  - Town Hall 2 or higher.
  - The tutorial is done.
  - At least 3 hours since the last raid, or since the village was founded.
  - The owner has been online for 20 minutes.
- **Warning.** A 10-minute warning follows the announcement, so no raid ever lands in your first half hour online.
- **Postponing.** Logging out during the countdown, or being offline when it ends, postpones the raid. It's never fired at an empty village.
- **Where it runs.** The raid runs in the realm room. The room is started if nobody's in Avatar Mode, and the reaper won't close a room mid-raid.
- **Pathing.** Raiders follow a BFS flow field from the Storehouse's free edge tiles (or the Town Hall's if there's no Storehouse). They walk in through the gate and break off to fight anyone within 4.5 tiles.
- **Plots.** Raiders may step into the plot they're raiding, and only that one. Every other monster still treats villages as safe.
- **Rosters.** Raid rosters must be melee walkers. The loader rejects shooters, ambushers and summoners: shots don't hit guards, and an ambusher would just hide in your village.

### D-098: guards and watchtowers
**Decision.**
- **Guards.** Guards are the villagers posted at a Guard Post (1/1/2/2/3 slots by level). They're named, and get +15% HP and damage per post level. They fight raiders within 6 tiles of their post and leash at 10.
- **Before Phase 14.** Before villagers existed, every slot was filled by a nameless guard. That fallback remains for village states without villagers.
- **Rendering.** Guards travel as monster entities with flag 128 (friendly), so the client draws them like monsters but with green HP bars.
- **Fallen guards.** They're back for the next raid.
- **Watchtowers.** They loose an arrow every 1.2 seconds at the nearest raider within 7 tiles, for 6/9/13/18/24 damage by level. The arrow is drawn on the client from a `TOWER` event.
- **"Defend!"** The button, in either mode, hops you just inside your own gate during a countdown or a raid. From Build Mode it first steps you into the world.

### D-099: theft, rewards and the report
**Decision.**
- **Theft.** Each raw resource loses 10% × (raiders through ÷ raiders sent). The raid is held if fewer than half got through. Buildings are never touched.
- **Rewards for a held raid.**
  - 60 coins × Town Hall level.
  - A resource bundle.
  - A 25% chance at the raid biome's rare material. It drops at your feet if you're in the world, otherwise it goes to your bag.
- **Raiders.** They give XP but no loot: the raid pays out at the end.
- **Report.** It names the outcome (flawless, held or overrun), the funny line (`{res}` is the most-stolen resource), what went and what came, how many guards are still standing, and the realm-mates who helped.
- **Deferred.** Walls and gates wait for a later phase. Gaps in buildings already make chokepoints, and a wall that blocks villagers and players needs a gate rule (P-later).

## Phase 14: villagers and beauty

### D-100: villagers arrive and leave lazily
**Decision.** Villagers are settled on every village read, like production.
- **Arrivals.** The clock walks forward in 10-minute steps, and each step brings a newcomer if there's a free bed and happiness is 40 or more. A new village starts with two.
- **Leaving.** After 2 hours below happiness 20, the newest arrival leaves. The panel counts down to it.
- **Food (P10).** Villagers eat food as a *rate*: 4/h each comes off the food rate, floored at zero, so nobody starves.
- **Approximation.** Arrivals that happen while you're away change food demand from the next read on, not from the moment they arrived. It's a small error that keeps settlement simple.

### D-101: jobs are a bonus, not a requirement
**Decision.** Buildings produce without workers, so existing villages don't suddenly halve. Each worker adds:
- **Production buildings.** +15% output each.
- **Crafting stations.** 10% faster crafts each (at most −50%).
- **Guard Post.** A guard for raids.

Assignment:
- **Slots.** They come from `jobs` on the building, or `villagers.yaml` by category.
- **Newcomers.** They take the open job with the highest priority (Guard Post first). A villager you give the day off stays off.
- **How to assign.** A dropdown per villager, or (desktop) drag a villager card onto a building on the map.

### D-102: how beauty is scored
**Decision.** A village's beauty is the sum of:
- each decoration's points
- 1.5 per *distinct* decoration type
- 2 per building linked to the Town Hall by paths: a breadth-first walk over path tiles from the Town Hall's edges
- 0.5 per tree, bush or weed still growing on the plot, so clearing everything costs a little beauty

Happiness follows the formula in BALANCE §10.
- **Tavern access.** A finished Tavern within 12 tiles (centre to centre, Manhattan distance) of more than half the homes. That's simpler than path distance, and close enough at plot scale.

### D-103: decorations are buildings
**Decision.** Decorations are entries in `buildings.yaml` with `category: decoration`, rather than the separate `village_decorations` and `village_tiles` tables SCHEMA sketched.
- **Behaviour.**
  - One level, no builder.
  - Placed instantly.
  - Moved, demolished and undone like any building.
  - Counted by Town Hall limits.
- **Why.** Placement, previews, undo, refunds and rendering all work unchanged.
- **Paths.** They're walkable 1×1 decorations. They don't block the world, and the client draws them on the ground layer.
- **Placing.** A decoration stays in hand after you place it, so you can lay a whole path or row of flowers.
- **Blueprint decorations.** The four from delves each use up one blueprint. Undo and demolish don't give it back.

### D-104: villagers walk their day on the client
**Decision.** Villagers are drawn and walked entirely on the client, from the village state and the world clock. The server only knows who lives and works where.
- **Daily routine.**
  - Dawn and day: they work near their job.
  - Dusk: they gather at the Tavern, or take a stroll.
  - Night: they go home and inside.
  - During a raid: they run home.
- **Pathing.** Routes use Dijkstra over the plot, with path tiles cheaper than grass, so paths get walked.
- **Speech bubbles.** They come from `villagers.yaml`, chosen by the village's weakest happiness factor (hungry, crowded, ugly, bored, leaving) or "happy" and "working".
- **Greetings.** Walk your avatar past and they stop, turn and greet you, sometimes admiring your weapon.
- **Replaces.** The Phase 3 ambient walkers, which had random looks and no jobs.

### D-105: friends are global, blocks are symmetric
**Decision.** Friendships cross realms; realm-mates don't need to befriend each other to visit.
- **One row per pair.** `friendships(user_low, user_high)` with `requested_by`, so a pair can't double up. Asking someone who already asked you accepts on the spot.
- **Blocks.** A block ends the friendship and silences both ways: no requests, no visits, no gifts, no chat bubbles or pings in the room, whoever blocked whom. The room reloads both players' block sets on the spot.
- **Cap.** 50 friends (`social.yaml: friends.max`).

### D-106: mail is a table, not a container
**Decision.** Parcels (gifts, boss loot you missed, login rewards that didn't fit) live in a `mail` table with `items` and `coins`, not the mailbox container SCHEMA sketched.
- **Claiming.** Items go into the bag; whatever doesn't fit stays in the parcel; coins go to the wallet. A parcel is gone once it's empty.
- **Why.** No slot juggling for a parcel nobody opened, and a whole bundle (coins plus three stacks) is one row with one Claim button.
- **Keeping.** 30 days and 50 parcels (`social.yaml: mail`).

### D-107: gifts, likes and help are small and gated (P4)
- **Gifts.** One per friend a day. Stackable materials, food and potions only, never gear. Up to 4 stacks, ~300 coins of market value per gift and 900 a day, valued with the stash's `unit_value`. Sending needs the P4 gate (level 5 or a day-old account).
- **Likes.** One per village, for good. A like from below the gate is recorded but doesn't count, so it can start counting later without a second like.
- **Neighbour help.** −25% of the time left on a building timer. One help per timer per day (from anyone), one per building per helper per day, 10 helps a day per helper, and helps never take more than half of a timer (`helped_seconds`). The helper gets 10 coins. The scheduled completion event moves with the timer.
- **Visits.** Friends and realm-mates. A visitor sees the village without its purse, undo, jobs or new discoveries.

### D-108: chat is presets only
**Decision.** Avatar Mode chat is emotes and eight quick phrases from `social.yaml`, sent by index. No free text.
- **Why.** No moderation queue, no profanity filter on the hot path, nothing to report, and it works across languages later. It's a cozy game; "Thanks!" and a heart cover most of it.
- **Reach.** Bubbles reach players within 30 tiles; pings reach the whole realm for 8 s. One bubble a second and one ping every 3 s per player.

### D-109: perks are server-side only
**Decision.** Skill perks change damage, crit, defence, HP, coins, gather yield, rare odds, regrowth, forge luck and time, repair cost and wear. None touch movement or attack timing.
- **Why.** The client predicts movement and swings with the shared rules (D-079). Perks that changed them would break prediction.
- **Learning.** Nodes need their `requires` at rank 1; capstones also need points spent in the branch. The first respec is free, then 40 coins per level.

### D-110: quests snapshot a counter, and a board never changes mid-period
**Decision.** Daily and weekly quests read the same `user_stats` counters as achievements.
- **Snapshot.** When a quest is first shown in a period, its counter's value is saved as `base`; progress is `value − base`. Nothing needs to hook every action.
- **Stable board.** The picks are seeded by `(user, period, start)`, filtered by level. Once a period's quests exist they're reused, so levelling past a quest's `min_level` mid-day doesn't swap the board and wipe progress. (A bug the screenshot run caught.)
- **Build stats.** `settle_village` is sync, so build counters are bumped when an upgrade *starts* (`build.upgrade`), not when it finishes.
- **Combos.** `discover.combo` is counted from the discoveries table, not a counter.
- **Main questline.** One chapter at a time: a counter, a Town Hall level or a villager count.

### D-111: XP rewards go through the room
**Decision.** Quest XP goes through `room.grant_xp` when the player is in a room, so the level-up event, HP and perks update live; otherwise straight to the database.

### D-112: the login calendar waits for you
**Decision.** 28 days that advance only when you claim. Missing a day loses nothing. Titles on days 7, 14, 21 and 28, cosmetic tokens along the way, resources into the village (allowed over the cap, never lost), items into the bag (or the mail if it's full).

### D-113: Phase 16 split in two
**Decision.** Phase 16a shipped the skill tree, quests, the main questline, achievements and titles, the collection book and the login calendar. Pets, mounts, fishing, photo mode and the Trophy Hall are Phase 16b. Cosmetic tokens pile up until 16b gives them a shop.

### D-114: pets are perks with legs; mounts are a speed multiplier
**Decision.** A pet is one small perk (≤10%, the same keys as the skill tree, D-109) plus a sprite that trots after you. It doesn't fight. A mount multiplies your walking speed while you ride.
- **Why no fighting pets.** A pet that attacks needs AI, targeting, balance and a network entity. A perk gets the same "my fox helps me" feeling, and other players still see your pet follow you (the roster carries it).
- **Getting them.** Two pets cost cosmetic tokens (finally something to spend them on); three are unlocked by counters (300 slimes, the Frost Matriarch, the Cinder Tyrant). The horse costs 2,500 coins and needs Town Hall 3; the ram costs 6 tokens.
- **Riding.** G toggles it. Speed goes through MOVE_MODS like plate armour and swift food, so prediction stays exact (D-079). The room throws you off in a fight, in a delve, when you're KO'd and when you cast a line.
- **Cut.** There's no Stable building: mounts are bought from the Journal's Pets tab. It would need a new building's art and adds nothing to play.

### D-115: fishing is server-timed
**Decision.** The server rolls the fish (by biome, season and time of day), picks when it bites and where the gold zone sits, and checks the press against its own clock.
- **The press.** The client reports how far along the bar its marker was. The server accepts it if it's in the zone (±60 ms of slack) and arrived no more than 0.9 s after it was made (network, and slow frames on phones; the screenshot run showed 600 ms was too tight).
- **What it can't stop.** A bot that reads the zone from the bite event can press perfectly. That's acceptable: fish are worth 8–200 coins at the NPC market, under its soft caps.
- **Where.** Water, ice in the Frozen North and lava in the Ashen Wastes. Village ponds count. Walking off reels the line in.
- **Records.** Every catch has a weight; your heaviest of each species is a `best.fish.<key>` counter kept at its maximum (`stats.record`). No new table.

### D-116: one source of truth for fish
**Decision.** `fishing.yaml` lists the species once. The loader makes each one a stackable bag item (category `fish`) and an NPC market entry priced from the species; the art generator draws an icon per species from its two colours.

### D-117: the Trophy Hall is a Journal tab
**Decision.** Boss trophies, biggest catches and lifetime totals live in the Journal, not in a building.
- **Why.** A hall building needs new art, a placement slot and a way to put things on shelves. The tab shows the same pride for none of that cost.

### D-118: photo mode is client-only
**Decision.** P hides the HUD, offers CSS filters (Golden, Dusk, Old map, Ink), toggles names and a paper frame, and saves a PNG made from Pixi's renderer (`extract.canvas`) with the same filter. You can keep walking while framing the shot.

### D-119: the player market sells stackables from escrow
**Decision.** Players list stackable goods (materials, food, potions, fish, trophies, blueprints, tokens) at a unit price. Listing takes the goods out of your bag into the listing row; nobody can sell the same goods twice.
- **Buying.** Partial buys are fine. The buyer pays at once and the seller is paid at once, less a 5% fee (at least 1 coin): the fee is a coin sink. Goods go to the buyer's bag; what doesn't fit goes by mail.
- **Races.** Every buy and cancel locks the listing row; both wallets are locked in user-id order so crossing trades can't deadlock; every request carries an idempotency key. A test fires two buyers at the last three units at once: exactly one gets them and no coins go missing.
- **Abuse (P4).** Listing needs the new-account gate. Prices must sit between 0.25× and 4× the item's reference value (its NPC market price, or the stash's value), so alts can't wash coins through silly prices. Ten listings a player.
- **Endings.** Listings last 48 h; expired and cancelled goods come back by mail, swept every 15 s by the global loop.
- **Cut.** No gear: instances would need their own escrow and inspection UI. That's the obvious next step.

### D-120: the world boss is one HP pool in Postgres
**Decision.** A world boss event row holds `hp_remaining` for the whole world. Every realm's room spawns the boss (the Stone Colossus's brain and body, enlarged and moonlit) at its hub.
- **Sync.** The room tallies its players' damage; every second its flush runs `hp_remaining = GREATEST(0, hp_remaining − tally)` and takes the result as the boss's HP. Other realms' damage shows up within a second or two. No HP scaling by fighters: the pool is sized for the world (300k × realms active in the last week; BALANCE's 2M is far too much for a small server).
- **The end.** Whichever update empties the pool marks the event defeated; rooms see it on their next read and the boss falls everywhere. Events also end when the weekend does. The global loop then pays each fighter by damage tier (×1.5 coins if it fell), by mail, exactly once (`rewarded`), and titles the fighters of the top ten realms "Worldbreaker".
- **When.** Saturday and Sunday (UTC). `POST /api/dev/worldboss` starts one any time for testing.

### D-121: leaderboards are lifetime counters, cached
**Decision.** Global boards read existing data: village likes, level (then XP), and `user_stats` counters (monsters, fish, delves, helps). The top 50 is cached for a minute; your own rank is one count query.
- **Cut.** No weekly boards yet: they need per-week counters or snapshots. The daily delve board (Phase 12) stays where it is.

### D-122: festivals are date windows in config
**Decision.** A festival runs between two dates (MM-DD, UTC, may wrap the new year). While it runs, every monster kill rolls its token for each helper (double for elites), and its shop sells titles, cosmetic tokens, food and potions for tokens. Four are configured: Spring Bloom, Midsummer Lanterns, Harvest Moon and Winter Hearth. A chip under the mini-map shows the one that's on.
- **Cut.** No festival decorations or world dressing yet (those need art); Phase 18's polish pass can add particles and a palette tint.


### D-123: one origin for the page, the API and the socket
**Decision.** In production the API serves the built frontend itself (`HF_STATIC_DIR`), so the page, `/api` and `/ws` share one origin. This settles D-002 / PLAN B2 without needing CORS or cross-site cookies.
- **Why.** Safari drops cross-site cookies, and a split `pages.dev` + `onrender.com` deploy would log every iPhone player out. One origin needs no domain to work (a custom domain is still nicer) and the client keeps its relative `/api` and `location.host/ws` URLs.
- **Caching.** Vite's hashed bundles are served for a year (`immutable`), generated art and fonts for an hour, and `index.html` never, so a deploy shows on the next load. Responses over 1 KB are gzipped.
- **Shipping.** A two-stage `Dockerfile` (Node builds the frontend; Python serves it) and a `render.yaml` blueprint: one web service (exactly one instance, since rooms live in memory, D-003), one worker from the same image, and Render Postgres. The web service runs `alembic upgrade head` before it starts.
- **Optional.** If the static files should come from Cloudflare Pages, `frontend/functions/_middleware.ts` proxies `/api` and `/ws` to the API (`API_ORIGIN`), which keeps it one origin to the browser. `_headers` and `_redirects` cover caching and SPA routes there.

### D-124: one keymap, rebindable, and a gamepad
**Decision.** Every keyboard action lives in `game/keymap.ts` with defaults; Settings → Controls rebinds them (click, press the new key; each key does one thing) and saves only the changed ones to `user_settings.keybinds`.
- **A bug it fixed.** Phase 16a bound the Journal to J, which was already the keyboard light attack: in the world, J swung and opened the Journal at once. The Journal is now L ("log").
- **Gamepads.** The browser's "standard" mapping (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro): left stick walks, right stick aims, A or RT attacks, B dodges, X heavy, Y ability, LB offhand, RB potion, LT interacts (and reels in a fish), the D-pad picks quick slots, Start opens the bag and Back the map. It feeds the same input as the keyboard, so prediction doesn't care. Build Mode stays mouse, touch and keyboard.

### D-125: phones get a More menu
**Decision.** Below 640 px the Build bar keeps Build, Market, Journal, More and Explore; the Avatar top bar keeps Party, Chat, Bag, Village and More. The rest (Recipes, Friends, Plaza, Realm, Photo, zoom, Ride, Settings) moves into a More popover that carries their notification dots.
- **Why.** At 390 px the bars ran off both edges of the screen: Explore, and in the world the Village button home, were unreachable on phones.

### D-126: generative music
**Decision.** A small generative score (`game/music.ts`), synthesized like the effects (D-047) and following the Music slider: a plucked major-pentatonic tune over I–vi–IV–V by day, a sparser minor one at night, and a soft heartbeat pulse in a fight. Pentatonic so nothing clashes, and random walks so it never quite repeats. Quiet on purpose: it sits under the ambient beds.

### D-127: the admin panel is a page in the client
**Decision.** `/admin` (role=admin) shows the server at a glance (players, online, realms, coin supply and its faucets and sinks, the player market, events, every room's tick times), player search and detail (counters, bag, the last 25 ledger lines), and actions: grant coins and items, ban and unban (a ban also pulls them out of the world), mark test accounts, start a world boss. Every action lands in `admin_audit_log` with who did it. `hf make-admin NAME` makes an admin.
- **A bug it found.** Signup never set `last_seen_at`, so new players counted as inactive for their first five minutes (and the NPC market's population scaling and the world boss's HP undercounted them). Signup sets it now.

### D-128: performance budgets in CI, a load test in tools
**Decision.** `tools/budget.mjs` fails CI when the shipped bundle grows past its budgets (docs/PERF.md). `tools/loadtest.py` puts realms of bots in the world at 20 Hz and reports the server's tick times and what the bots felt. The F3 overlay shows the client's frame rate, frame time, sprites on stage, entities and ping against the same budgets.
